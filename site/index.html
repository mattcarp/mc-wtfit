What the fuck is this?

Point your phone at the thing in the drawer. WTFIT tells you what it is, whether it belongs in your life, and if it doesn't, turns it into money for someone who needs it more than you do.

Get early access See how it decides

Cables. Hubs. The fourth projector. A fondue set from 2009. The rowing machine that became a coat rack. Anything.

01 — How it decides

Three possible answers. Never a shrug.

A

Keep.

It's useful, and here's exactly where it fits: the project, the room, the job it does. You finally know why you own it.

→ Saved to your inventory
B

Build.

It gets interesting next to things you already own. That control deck, meet the projector in the spare room: one button, one cinema.

→ Saved as an idea
C

Let it go.

No place in your life. A place in someone else's, maybe. WTFIT writes the listing, prices it, and sends the money where it's needed.

→ Listed; proceeds to charity

Every photo also becomes an entry in your inventory. A weekend of snapping and you have a catalogue of everything you own, in every house, and far better ideas for what to build with it.

02 — The one rule

The money goes to someone more vulnerable than you.

When WTFIT can't find a place for something in your life, it helps you sell it. By default, every cent goes to Animal Welfare Gozo, who rescue and rehome injured strays across the island. WTFIT is open source, so the choice is yours: pick any charity you like. But when in doubt, give it to the animals.

03 — Principles

Decisive, not nosy.

Your key, your data

Bring your own AI key. Your inventory and context stay with you.

Asks only what helps

Your projects, skills and gear. Nothing a recommendation doesn't need.

Unsure means retake

Low confidence asks for the label or the connector, never a blind sale.

Wiped before it ships

Anything with storage or an account gets a wipe-and-unlink checklist.

You confirm every sale

WTFIT suggests. Nothing is listed until you say so.
