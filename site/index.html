01 — How it decides

Never a shrug.

Four questions, asked in order, against what WTFIT knows about your projects, your skills and the gear you already own. The first answer that sticks is the verdict.

  1. Q1Can we tell what it is?If not →RetakeShoot the label, the connector or the serial plate. Unsure never means sell.
  2. Q2Does it already have a job in your life?If yes →KeepHere's the job it does, and it goes into your inventory.
  3. Q3Could it pair with something you own?If yes →BuildHere's what the two of them make together.
  4. Q4Otherwise.Always →Let it goListed, priced, and the money goes to charity.

03 — The one rule

The money goes to someone more vulnerable than you.

When WTFIT can't find a place for something in your life, it helps you sell it, and by default every cent goes to Animal Welfare Gozo, who rescue and rehome injured strays across the island.

WTFIT is open source, so the choice is yours: any charity you like. But when in doubt, give it to the animals.

04 — Principles

Decisive, not nosy.

It needs to know your projects and your gear. It does not need to know anything else about you, so it doesn't ask.

P1

Your key, your data

Bring your own AI key. Your inventory and context stay with you.

P2

Asks only what helps

Projects, skills and gear. Nothing a recommendation doesn't need.

P3

Unsure means retake

Low confidence asks for the label or connector, never a blind sale.

P4

Wiped before it ships

Anything with storage or an account gets a wipe-and-unlink checklist.

P5

You confirm every sale

WTFIT suggests. Nothing is listed until you say so.
